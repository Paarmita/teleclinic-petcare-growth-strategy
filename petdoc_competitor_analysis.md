# PetDoc — Competitor Landscape & USP

**Bottom line:** Competitors mostly sell the same thing PetDoc does: a good one-off video consult. Almost none visibly own what happens *after* the call, and that is exactly where PetDoc loses users (only ~21% return within 180 days).

> Note: PetDoc is the fictional case company, so these are real-world comparables. Public detail on competitor features and prices was limited; the "weak spot" column reflects their positioning, not a full feature audit.

## 1. Competitor landscape (Germany first)

| Type | Examples | How they compete | Weak spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct D2C tele-vet apps** | Dr. Fressnapf (Fressnapf group), FirstVet, Pfotendoctor, Dr. Sam | Video / chat / phone. Pay per consult or flat rate (from ~€30 per quarter). Pitched on 24/7 availability, no waiting room | Each consult is a separate transaction; Pfotendoctor positions itself as an after-hours option |
| **Insurers bundling tele-vet for free** | Allianz, DA Direkt, Barmenia (with FirstVet), Dalma | Free video consults as a policy perk | Commoditises the consult itself; a hotline, not a relationship |
| **Retail / pharmacy ecosystems** | Fressnapf, Shop-Apotheke | Consult + shopping + medication | Strong on delivery and commerce, weak on continuity of care |
| **Local clinics/chains + US/EU models** | Local vets; Airvet, Pawp, Vetster abroad | Clinics hold the record and the relationship; Airvet offers unlimited follow-ups via subscription | Clinics aren't digital-first; subscription is off the table for PetDoc (pricing fixed in the brief) |

## 2. Where PetDoc can't win

- **Price:** insurers give consults away for free.
- **24/7 availability and convenience:** every competitor already claims it.
- **Consult quality:** 4.8★ and NPS 65 show the first visit is already good. It's the minimum to compete, not a reason to return.

## 3. Where PetDoc can win (USPs)

### USP 1 — "The vet already knows my pet" (continuity)
- **Why:** re-telling cuts return by about a third; for chronic owners it drops from 54.5% to 34.6%.
- **Gap:** insurer hotlines and pay-per-call apps start from zero every time; clinics have the history but aren't on demand.
- **Defensibility:** every consult, upload and prescription makes the pet record more valuable — a data moat a free hotline can't copy quickly.

### USP 2 — Closed-loop follow-up (day-3 check-in, same vet)
- **Why:** acute owners are 61% of users and return only 14.3%; nobody checks in on them.
- **Gap:** the closest precedent is Airvet's unlimited follow-ups, behind a subscription. PetDoc can offer a vet-set check-in inside pay-per-visit, within the fixed pricing.
- **Effect:** moves the relationship from "I call when panicking" to "PetDoc follows up on my pet."

### USP 3 — Proactive care plan for young animals
- **Why:** this segment returns at 25.7% and has predictable needs (vaccines, deworming, check-ups).
- **Gap:** clinics send reminders but offer no digital booking at that moment; tele-vet apps only react when something is wrong.
- **Result:** PetDoc becomes the app owners open between problems, not only during them.

### Supporting differentiator (not a USP) — Integrated prescription journey
Delivery tracking and dose reminders help (each extra delivery day: OR 0.85 on return), but Fressnapf and Shop-Apotheke are stronger on logistics. Aim for parity, not leadership.

## 4. Positioning

> *Others give you a vet when something's wrong. PetDoc is the vet who remembers your pet and checks back.*

Maps to the top bets: B1/B2 pet profile + vet summary, A1 day-3 check-in, C1 care plan.

**Main risk:** Fressnapf has the reach and customer relationship to copy continuity features. PetDoc's defence is speed and building up the pet record and same-vet relationships first.
