From one consultation to continuous care

PetDoc's first visit is loved (4.8★, NPS 65), but only about 1 in 5 owners comes back. Here's why, what we'd build to fix it, and how we'd know it's working, based on 1,413 consultations from 1,200 pet owners.

01

The brief

PetDoc is Germany's leading pet telemedicine app: video and chat with vets, with prescriptions shipped through a partner pharmacy. The quarter's goal for the Patient Experience mission is fixed: more users come back for a second consultation.

Scale

50,000 MAU

~2,800 new registrations a month, 120 vets on the platform

Quality

4.8★ · NPS 65

The first visit is already good

Economics

€35–45 / visit

~€10 contribution margin per consultation. Pricing is fixed.

Problem

23% return

Second consultation within 180 days (brief). 21.2% at owner level in the sample.

What was asked

  • Feature concept: which problem, for whom, with quantified impact; the feature; who to involve; how to validate and what would prove it wrong.
  • Working prototype of the moment where the hypothesis is decided.
  • Screen recording walking through what was discarded, chosen and why.

The lens: how the role is framed

  • Engagement and retention as North Star metrics; an app owners open "again and again", not just once.
  • End-to-end discovery: real problems from data and feedback, hypotheses tested before building.
  • Work across missions: Patient Experience owns the journey around the consultation. Vet Experience and Growth own adjacent parts.
  • A trio (PM, Engineering Manager, Product Designer) with nine engineers including QA.

03

The problem

PetDoc treats each consultation as a one-off transaction, while owners need ongoing care for their pet.

Nothing pulls them back after the visit, and nothing remembers them when they return.

Layer 1 · The bigger gap

No reason to return

Acute owners return at 14%, and only 2.2% within 30 days. Even a perfect visit only gets acute to ~17%. The gap is after the visit, not in it.

Layer 2 · The trust breaker

No memory when they return

19% of visits involve re-telling. It cuts return from 23% to 15% (chronic 55% → 35%) and is hidden by ratings.

Personas

Primary target

Worried one-timer

Acute · 61% · 14.3% return

"When my pet is still unwell a few days after the call, I want to know whether this is normal and get the same vet to look again quickly."

Secondary target

New pet parent

Young animal · 22% · 25.7% return

"I've just got a puppy and don't know what's due when. Tell me what my pet needs and when."

Protect

Care manager

Chronic · 12% · 51.0% return

"I want every vet to already know the history, meds and last results, so we spend the time on what's changed."

Internal

PetDoc vet

120 vets

"When a consultation starts, I want a one-screen view of the pet's history and what's new, without making the call longer."

What this rules out: fixing video, wait time or platform alone. Together they're worth ~+4pp, which is necessary but won't reach the quarter's goal. Unclear (5%, 11.1%) is too small to optimise for now.

05

Market and positioning

Competitors mostly sell the same thing PetDoc does: a good one-off video consult. Almost none visibly own what happens after the call, which is exactly where PetDoc loses users.

TypeExamplesHow they competeWeak spot
Direct D2C tele-vet appsDr. Fressnapf, FirstVet, Pfotendoctor, Dr. SamVideo / chat / phone; pay per consult or flat rate from ~€30 per quarter; 24/7Each consult is a separate transaction
Insurers bundling tele-vetAllianz, DA Direkt, Barmenia (FirstVet), DalmaFree video consults as a policy perkCommoditises the consult; a hotline, not a relationship
Retail / pharmacyFressnapf, Shop-ApothekeConsult + shopping + medicationStrong on delivery, weak on continuity of care
Clinics + US/EU modelsLocal vets; Airvet, Pawp, VetsterClinics hold the record; Airvet offers unlimited follow-ups via subscriptionClinics aren't digital-first; subscription is off the table

Where PetDoc can't win

  • Price: insurers give consults away for free.
  • 24/7 convenience: every competitor already claims it.
  • Consult quality: 4.8★ is the minimum to compete, not a reason to return.

Where PetDoc can win

  • "The vet already knows my pet": every consult, upload and prescription makes the record more valuable. A data moat a free hotline can't copy quickly.
  • Closed-loop follow-up: a vet-set check-in inside pay-per-visit, within the fixed pricing.
  • Proactive care plan for young animals: the app owners open between problems.
  • Supporting: integrated Rx journey. Aim for parity, not leadership.

"Others give you a vet when something's wrong. PetDoc is the vet who remembers your pet and checks back."

Main risk: Fressnapf has the reach to copy continuity features. PetDoc's defence is speed, and building up pet records and same-vet relationships first.

Acceptance criteria

  • Vet sets or confirms a check-in date during the consultation (default by condition type).
  • Owner gets "How is [pet] today?" with better / same / worse.
  • Same or worse → one-tap re-check with the same vet, history attached; fallback to the next available vet.
  • Better closes the case and saves it to the pet record.

Built on Pillar 1

  • B1 thin profile: every consult, upload and prescription saved automatically.
  • B2 vet summary: last 3 consults, active meds, latest uploads and the owner's "what's new", on one screen before joining.
  • B7 survey: one-tap "the vet already knew my pet" after every visit, to see what ratings hide.

09

Validation: how we'd know we're wrong

Each bet is A/B tested per persona, with a holdout group kept all year to measure the combined effect. The 180-day north star lags, so leading metrics drive go / no-go decisions.

Scale if

End-of-Q1 exit gate

  • Check-in response ≥ 30%
  • Re-checks within 14 days ≥ 3%
  • Vets mark < 15% of re-checks "not needed"
  • Re-telling trending from 17–19% toward < 10%
Signs we'd be wrong

And what we'd do

  • Owners respond but don't book: they want reassurance, not a re-check → bring free async follow-up (A4) forward.
  • Care plans set up, bookings go to local clinics → position PetDoc as "plan & triage", explore clinic partnerships.
  • Re-telling falls, return doesn't move: it was a symptom, not the cause → look at vet quality and consistency.
  • Opt-outs rise faster than returns → cap frequency, make check-ins opt-in by condition.
Guardrails

Must not get worse

  • App Store rating ≥ 4.8, NPS ≥ 65
  • Notification opt-out / uninstall rate within agreed limit
  • Consultation length and vet satisfaction stable
  • Share of re-checks marked "not needed" (clinical appropriateness)
  • Contribution margin ~€10 per consultation, no discount-driven returns
Confidence: all figures come from 1,200 owners, so they show direction rather than precise outcomes. Care managers (149 owners) and Unclear (54) have wide error margins. Rating is missing for 54% of first visits and raters return more often (24% vs 19%), so ratings are biased upward. An owner's first visit in the sample may not be their true first visit.

Who we involve before building

Good concepts touch other missions. This is what Patient Experience needs from each, and how we'd get it.

Our mission

The trio + team

EM and Product Designer co-own discovery and the prototype. Nine engineers including QA size the thin profile (critical path) and the notification / booking work.

Vet Experience

Sign-off before ship

Co-design the pre-call summary and the check-in date setting with vets. The summary must fit the workflow without making consultations longer; vets confirm whether a re-check is clinically needed.

Growth

Shared ownership

Agree who owns the care-plan sign-up flow. Higher lifetime value per owner directly helps their acquisition economics. Partnerships (clinics, pharmacy) in Q4.

Data

Measurement

North star per persona, the "vet knew my pet" score, monthly cohort review, A/B tests per persona and a year-long holdout.

Missions owning friction

Track and push

Connection check + audio fallback (D2), reliable uploads (D3). Worth ~+3pp combined; necessary but not ours.

Ops and pharmacy partner

Delivery

Rx delivery ≤ 4 days (each extra day: OR 0.85 on return). Delivery tracking and dose reminders on our side.
